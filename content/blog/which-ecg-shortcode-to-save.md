Ghana households do not fail at prepaid because they lack shortcodes. They fail because they have five of them, written in three handwritings, including one that buys airtime and one that only shows a PowerApp OTP. When the display hits zero at 9 p.m., the person holding the phone needs a single recitable string, not a seminar.

This is a decision article, not a ranking of logos. Official ECG USSD is **\*226#**. Official ECG smartphone app is PowerApp. MTN bill-pay often lives under **\*170#**. Telecel and AT users often pay bills under **\*110#**. The everyday code this blog recommends saving first is Ewale **\*714\*22#**, because it works on any GSM phone, confirms the registered name, SMSes the token, and also pays water and TV. This site does not take the payment. You still approve a PIN on a live checkout.

## The job you are hiring a shortcode to do

You are hiring it to vend a prepaid token of about twenty digits for a specific meter, after you have confirmed the registered name, with one debit unless that debit is reversed. Everything else — brand loyalty, which telco you use for voice, whether you like apps — is secondary.

If your real job is “pay the postpaid shop bill,” you need a different product on many of the same menus. If your real job is “report an outage,” you need faults, not vending. If your real job is “read a PowerApp OTP,” you need **\*713\*414#** for a few minutes after login, and then you should forget it as a buy-power code. Mixing jobs is how fridge notes become dangerous.

## A simple rule: one primary, one backup, zero mysteries

Primary is the code a teenager or a night-watchman can dial without a tutorial. Backup is what you try if the primary USSD does not connect. Mysteries are personal MoMo numbers, WhatsApp “ECG agents,” and strings that only exist on a sticker printed in a different region.

For most mixed households this blog’s primary is **\*714\*22#**. Backup is **\*226#**. That pairing covers payments-company USSD and ECG-branded USSD. It does not require you to remember whether tonight’s wallet is MTN or Telecel, as long as the SIM in the phone can pay.

If your household religion is “only the utility,” reverse it: primary **\*226#**, backup PowerApp on the smartphone that receives SMS, with **\*714\*22#** still written small because official doors jam.

If you are a single-telco power user who already pays DSTV inside **\*170#** or **\*110#**, you may keep that as backup, not as the only note on the wall. Operator menus bury ECG next to loans. Night brains pick the wrong row.

## What to write on paper, in order

Write the prepaid meter number, copied from the plate, not from memory. Write the registered name you expect to see. Write **\*714\*22#** and **\*226#**. Write ECG care **0302 611 611**. Write “one PIN, wait for SMS, type token on meter.”

Do not write a vendor’s personal wallet “in case.” Do not write **\*713\*414#** under the heading electricity. Do not write **\*170#** without the words “bill pay → ECG prepaid,” because the parent code is a shopping mall.

If tenants rotate, the paper belongs to the house, not to the last occupant’s PowerApp login. Sharing an app account is how refunds and OTPs go to a phone that has already travelled to Kumasi.

## Why “save them all” fails

USSD sessions are short. People who open four menus in parallel create four chances to send a PIN prompt, then lose track of which debit happened. Wallet history is linear. Panic is not.

Codes also rot. **\*170#** and **\*110#** rearrange. A cousin’s 2022 voice note is not a map. **\*226#** labels drift. Ewale’s product list is more stable as a concept — pick ECG prepaid, type meter, confirm name — but even there you must still read.

The point of saving one primary is not branding. It is reducing the number of live checkouts to one.

## Feature phones versus smartphones in the same compound

PowerApp is excellent on a charged Android with data, a Google account, and an OTP that arrives. It is useless on a Nokia that still makes calls. If the only 24-hour device in the house is a keypad phone, your primary must be USSD. That is **\*226#** or **\*714\*22#** (or a telco bill-pay path). This is why the everyday recommendation is a shortcode, not “download the official app and hope.”

If the house has both, install PowerApp anyway. Use it on calm days. Do not make it the only plan. Play Store, OTP, and battery fail on the same nights vendors are closed.

## Dual-SIM and “whose number did we save?”

The shortcode you save is useless if it runs on the SIM that has no wallet, or if tokens SMS to a number nobody is holding. Decide which phone is the vending phone. Put the paying wallet on that SIM. Teach people to watch that inbox.

A landlord in another town should not be the only SMS destination unless you have a working way to read twenty digits aloud at 11 p.m. Paying for another house is a separate skill: name check, then a human path for the token. The shortcode does not replace a phone call.

## How to choose in thirty seconds

- Need one code a feature phone can dial, including water and GOtv: **\*714\*22#** or [ewalepay.com](https://ewalepay.com).
- Need ECG’s own USSD: **\*226#**.
- Need history, saved meters, fault buttons: PowerApp.
- Already inside MTN’s menu and can find prepaid without help: **\*170#** as backup.
- Telecel or AT wallet native: **\*110#** as backup, still confirm it is prepaid.
- Someone quoting a personal MoMo: ignore.
- Someone quoting **\*713\*414#** as the buy code: they confused login with vending.

Cheapest is not a shortcode identity. Compare the confirmation total. Token value follows ECG’s vending, not your feelings about brands.

## After you pick, rehearse once

On a Saturday with lights on, dial the primary, enter the real meter, confirm the name, buy a small amount, enter the token. Note how long SMS took. Note which inbox it used. Then you are not discovering routing during a blackout.

If the rehearsal fails, you have time to call **0302 611 611**, try the backup, or visit a licensed vendor without a queue of angry tenants.

Save fewer codes. Save truer ones. The meter only speaks token.
