Ghana Water is allowed to be slow on the **statement**. It is not allowed to be ambiguous about whether Ewale took the money. You separate those with the wallet.

## A working timeline

- **T+0:** You complete *714*22# and approve PIN.
- **T+seconds to minutes:** Mobile money shows **completed**.
- **T+minutes:** Ewale treats the collection as paid.
- **T+hours (sometimes next cycle):** Ghana Water **posts** to the customer account.

If you check the bill at T+20 minutes, you are checking the wrong clock.

## What “up to 24 hours” means

It is a posting SLA, not a hope. Most payments appear sooner. Weekend and public-holiday cycles can sit at the long end. Plan disconnection-sensitive payments **before** the morning the crew is expected, not while they are at the gate — unless you can show a reference.

## At the gate

If a disconnection team arrives:

- Show mobile money **completed** debit.
- Show Ewale **reference**.
- Show the **account number** you paid.

They may still follow a printed list. Your documents are how you argue the list is stale. Paying a **second** time at the gate to “make the list happy” is how people pay twice in one day.

## When the payment is actually missing

No debit in the wallet after a closed prompt: **not paid**. Dial again.

Debit reversed later: treat as **not paid** and do not expect Ghana Water to post. If Ewale still shows paid, that is a reconciliation case for support.

Debit present, days later, still nothing on the account: **support + Ghana Water**, with the reference. That is past a normal batch window.

## Do not use estimated bills as the only source of truth

Estimated consumption and actual readings fight. Your Ewale payment still hits the **account**. It will not resolve a reading dispute. Pay what you intend to pay; fight the estimate on Ghana Water’s process.

\*714\*22# is the collection. The reference is the trail. The bill is a delayed view of the same money.
