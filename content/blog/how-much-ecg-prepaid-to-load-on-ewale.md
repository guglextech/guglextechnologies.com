On Ewale you type **GHS**. The meter displays **kWh**. Mixing them up leads to buying “50” and expecting 50 units.

## What your amount is

The figure on **\*714\*22#** is the collection amount in cedis. From that, ECG’s tariff (and any applicable charges) determines units. Those units change when tariffs change. Last year’s mental math is wrong.

If the SMS shows units, use that as the forecast for how long the load will last — not the cedis.

## How to choose an amount

- **Keep the lights on tonight:** a small confirmed amount is better than a failed large one. Buy again tomorrow if needed.
- **Cover the week:** look at how fast the meter falls on a normal week, then buy enough cedis that the **units** last — after you have seen one SMS conversion.
- **Avoid zero at 10 p.m.:** prepaid cuts hard. Do not run to the last 2 units if you cannot stand at the meter after dark.

There is no Ewale setting for “fill the meter.” You choose a number.

## Failed large payments

Wallet limits and daily caps kill large PIN prompts. If GHS 200 fails and GHS 50 works, you can make **multiple successful** purchases. Each is a **separate token**. Enter each token. Do not assume they merge before the keypad.

## Very small amounts

Tiny purchases still incur the same identification and SMS path. They are valid. They may be inefficient if you pay charges on the wallet side often. Fewer, slightly larger loads usually mean fewer chances to mistype a meter.

## After tariff changes

When ECG adjusts tariffs, the same GHS 100 buys fewer units. That will look like Ewale “giving less.” Check the units on the SMS before opening a support ticket about missing power.

## Record one conversion

Once, write down: amount paid, units received, date. That is your household’s only useful predictor. Until you have that, guess with a margin, not with optimism.

Pay on *714*22# in cedis. Enter tokens. Watch units on the meter. Those are three different numbers.
