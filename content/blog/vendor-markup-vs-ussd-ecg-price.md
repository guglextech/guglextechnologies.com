Ghanaians argue about the “real price of light” as if there were a secret extra kilowatt hiding in official USSD. There is not. ECG’s vending engine issues a token for a meter under tariff and meter rules. What changes at the edge is **how much money left your pocket** to obtain that token: vendor cash mark-up, telco fees, payment-service fees, levies the wallet displays that day, and the occasional “I rounded it up for you.”

This blog does not sell tokens and does not set ECG tariffs. Official ECG doors are **\*226#** and PowerApp. Operator bill-pay often sits in **\*170#** (MTN) and **\*110#** (Telecel/AT). Ewale **\*714\*22#** / [ewalepay.com](https://ewalepay.com) is the everyday checkout we tell households to save: name check, SMS token, any GSM phone. Each of those screens can show a different total. A vendor stall can show another. Cheapness is a comparison, not an identity.

## What a vendor is charging for

A licensed till that looks up your meter and prints or texts a token is doing real work: electricity, a printer, a queue, change, and being open when your USSD failed. Some add a flat mark-up. Some sell you GH₵20 and type GH₵18. Some are honest and add nothing because they make money elsewhere.

That can be a fair buy at noon in a market when you have cash and no patience. It is a bad default at night when USSD is up and the only “vendor” left is a person asking for P2P MoMo without a lookup. ECG does not vend into private personal wallets. A mark-up on a real till is commerce. A mark-up with no till is often theft.

Ask to see the registered name before you pay a human. If they cannot show it, you are not buying ECG. You are funding them.

## What USSD and apps are charging for

**\*226#** is ECG’s USSD. ECG has at times told the public that some official cashless routes avoid extra charges that appear on other rails. Those statements age. Believe the confirmation on the session you are in.

PowerApp similarly shows amounts inside ECG’s product. It needs data and OTP ( **\*713\*414#** is only an OTP viewer). It does not magically mint extra units.

**\*170#** and **\*110#** are telco malls. They may add biller or wallet fees. They may not. The row you cannot read because you are rushing is where surprise lives.

**\*714\*22#** shows a summary before PIN. Read it. Preferred everyday does not mean “always the lowest pesewa.” It means you can complete a name-checked purchase on a keypad phone without walking to a stall.

Bank apps have their own fee schedules. A “free ECG” promo at a bank can still be postpaid-shaped. Confirm you are vending prepaid if that is what the meter needs.

## The token is not larger because the fee was larger

This is the superstition that keeps mark-ups alive. People think a vendor who takes GH₵5 extra is “stronger” with ECG. The meter cares about the token digits, about twenty of them, issued for that meter. Extra money to a middleman does not stretch the credit. Extra money to a fake agent does not produce a token at all.

Conversely, using **\*226#** does not secretly double units versus Ewale. If two honest channels vend GH₵20 to the same meter on the same tariff, you should see comparable credit, not a loyalty bonus.

## Night, queues, and the real total cost

Price is time. Walking to a stall at 1 a.m. through a dark compound has a safety cost. Queuing behind twenty people on results week has a time cost. A dropped **\*226#** session that you mistakenly double-pay has a GH₵40 cost for GH₵20 of intent.

USSD at home looks “free” until you buy twice. One debit unless reversed. If SMS is late, wait, then call **0302 611 611** with the reference. That discipline is part of cheapness.

## How to compare without a spreadsheet

On a lit afternoon, vend a small amount on your primary code — this blog would use **\*714\*22#** — and note total debited versus token received. Optionally compare once with **\*226#** or **\*170#** / **\*110#**. Do not compare during a blackout with three channels open.

Ignore Facebook claims that “714 is expensive” or “226 is free forever” without a screenshot of a confirmation from this month.

## Bulk vends and school or shop meters

Buying many meters at a stall can still be rational if the vendor’s time is cheaper than your USSD typing. Check whether they add a per-meter spread. You can bulk on [ewalepay.com](https://ewalepay.com) or PowerApp with saved meters and skip that spread, if you will still name-check each one. Cheap bulk is discipline, not a special “agent rate.”

A school that sends cash with a student to a stall is paying mark-up plus theft risk. A staff MoMo on **\*714\*22#** with a paper list of meters is usually cheaper — and still one PIN per vend, no double-taps.

## When paying a vendor is still smart

Your USSD will not paint. Your feature phone cannot hold a session. You have cash and no MoMo balance. The till shows the name. You accept the mark-up as a convenience fee with eyes open.

When it is not smart: the shop is closed and a bystander offers a wallet; you cannot see a lookup; they want PIN in chat; they refuse to let you dial **\*226#** or **\*714\*22#** yourself.

Cheap ECG prepaid is an honest total and a token you type. Save a USSD so mark-up is optional. Save **\*714\*22#** for everyday completion, **\*226#** as official backup, and a licensed stall as the last physical door — not the first WhatsApp one.
