Ewale will sell a token to any valid meter you confirm. It will not judge whether that meter is on *your* wall. These are the mistakes that show up in support.

## 1. The meter on the pole, not in the house

In compounds, several prepaid meters sit in one box. The sticker you remember from last year may be the neighbour’s. Read the sticker **on the meter whose keypad you will use**.

## 2. Paying with a postpaid account number

Old ECG bills in a drawer have account numbers. Those are not prepaid meter numbers. Lookup may fail, or it may resolve a different product. If you are standing at a **keypad**, you need the **meter** number.

## 3. Pasting the last token as the meter

A token is long. A meter number is shorter. If you paste from an old SMS into the meter field on a later purchase, you are not identifying a meter.

## 4. Confirming a name you do not recognise

The lookup name is the control. “I will fix it later” is how tokens vanish. Hang up. Dial **\*714\*22#** again.

Landlords: the registered name may be yours while tenants pay. Tell them the name they must see. Tenants: if you see the landlord’s name, that can still be correct — confirm with them once, in person.

## 5. Paying from memory in the dark

Power is already out. You type what you *think* the last three digits were. Close enough is not a meter number.

Use a torch. Photograph the sticker in daylight when power is on, and keep that photo for the next outage. Do not photograph the token SMS on the same image as the sticker if you will share the photo — that combination is enough to load the meter.

## What Ewale will not undo automatically

Once mobile money settles and a token is issued, ECG’s vending system has produced digits for **that** meter. There is no self-service “switch meter” on *714*22#.

Prevention is the product: lookup, name, confirm, then PIN.

If you already paid the wrong meter, gather reference + both numbers and contact support. Do not buy a third token while you wait unless you are standing at the *correct* meter and treating it as a separate, intentional purchase.
