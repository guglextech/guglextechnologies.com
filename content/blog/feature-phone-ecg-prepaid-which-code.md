PowerApp is ECG’s official smartphone app. That sentence is useless in a room whose only working handset is a keypad phone with a torch. Feature phones cannot open Play Store, cannot hold a modern WebView, and often cannot keep a browser checkout alive. They can still dial USSD, receive SMS, and display about twenty token digits large enough to type on a meter.

This blog does not take payment. The preferred everyday shortcode we want on that Nokia is **\*714\*22#**: no data, name check, one mobile-money PIN, token SMS, and the same menu for Ghana Water and pay-TV. Official ECG USSD **\*226#** remains a correct door on the same keypad. MTN **\*170#** and Telecel/AT **\*110#** can work if someone in the house already knows those mazes. None of them require an app.

## Why “just download PowerApp” is a class of advice

It assumes Android, storage, a Google account, data, and an OTP SMS that arrives. ECG even documents **\*713\*414#** so you can read a missed PowerApp OTP — on a phone that requested the app login. A feature phone never requested that login. Writing **\*713\*414#** on the wall of a keypad-only house is clutter. It is not a shop.

Smartphones in the same compound may be out of battery, with the tenant, or locked. Planning vending around the fanciest device is how lights die when that device is elsewhere.

## Codes a feature phone can actually finish

**\*226#** — ECG-branded, data-free, prepaid if you pick the right row. Register the meter on a calm day if the menu allows. Sessions drop more on old handsets; stand where the signal is less ugly.

**\*170#** — MTN users. Bill-pay path to ECG. Many buttons. A senior who only ever used this menu for airtime will pick airtime again under stress unless the steps are written in huge letters: bill pay, ECG, prepaid, meter number, name, amount, PIN.

**\*110#** — Telecel/AT users. Same warning. Operator mall, not ECG HQ.

**\*714\*22#** — Ewale. This site’s everyday recommendation because the task list is short enough to teach: ECG, meter, name, amount, PIN, wait for SMS. [ewalepay.com](https://ewalepay.com) is the web twin if a smartphone with data appears later; it is not required tonight.

Licensed vendors with a till still serve people who will not dial anything. They may add a mark-up. That is a price for not using USSD, not a law of physics.

## SMS is the screen

On a feature phone the token SMS is the receipt and the product. Learn which SIM inbox to open. Dual-SIM Nokias still confuse people: USSD on SIM 1, SMS on SIM 2. Watch both, or remove the idle SIM before vending.

If the SMS is slow, the debit may still be real. One debit unless reversed. Opening **\*226#** because Ewale “did not text yet” is how you double pay. ECG care **0302 611 611** exists for missing tokens after a real payment, not as a first reflex after thirty seconds.

Type the digits on the meter with no spaces. Confirm key. If the backlight on the phone is dying, copy onto paper first. Do not let three people shout digits at once.

## Teaching a household that does not like menus

Write, in ink:

1. Meter number (from the plate).
2. Name we expect.
3. Dial **\*714\*22#** (or **\*226#** if that is your house rule).
4. ECG prepaid, not postpaid, not water.
5. If the name is wrong, cancel.
6. PIN once.
7. Token SMS → meter.

Add “ignore personal MoMo agents.” Feature-phone users are a favourite target for “I will load it for you, send to this number,” because they are assumed not to know USSD. ECG does not vend into private personal wallets.

## When the feature phone is not the paying wallet

Sometimes the Nokia can dial but the MoMo PIN lives on a different line. Then you need two people or two SIMs in one device: one to run USSD, one to approve PIN. If the prompt goes to a phone in another town, you will wait forever. Move the wallet SIM into the vending phone, or use a channel that prompts the wallet you actually control.

Bank apps are irrelevant on a true feature phone. So is PowerApp. Do not send the elder to town “because there is no app.” There is USSD.

## Teaching the elder without a smartphone lecture

Sit together. Let them dial **\*714\*22#**. Let them abort a deliberately wrong digit so they see cancel is allowed. Let them complete a small real vend. Write the steps in their language on paper, including “if name is not ours, stop.” Add **\*226#** as “ECG’s own code” so a neighbour cannot shame them for using Ewale.

Do not stack **\*170#** or **\*110#** on the same paper unless they already live there. Extra malls undo the lesson.

If SMS digits are hard to read, raise font if the Nokia allows, or copy to paper immediately. Care **0302 611 611** is a last step after a debit, not a tutor for the menu.

## Limits you should admit

Very old handsets struggle with long USSD. If **\*714\*22#** or **\*226#** never paints a menu, try outside, try another network SIM, or use a vendor till that looks up the name. Do not keep sending P2P to a helper.

If the meter is a card or NFC type that expects an app write, USSD token flow may not match that hardware. Most Ghana residential prepaid people are still in the twenty-digit STS-style world. If your device is exotic, ask ECG, not WhatsApp.

A keypad phone is enough for ordinary ECG prepaid. Save **\*714\*22#** as the code you can teach in one minute. Keep **\*226#** as the official USSD sibling. Leave PowerApp for the smartphones. The token does not know which of you is fashionable.
