The question “how long does ECG token take?” is really two questions. How long until the vending engine issues a code, and how long until your particular SIM shows the SMS. The first is usually short. The second is Ghana SMS: minutes, sometimes longer at night, sometimes immediately, sometimes on the other dual-SIM inbox.

This blog cannot ping ECG’s queue for you and does not take the payment. Whether you paid on PowerApp, **\*226#**, **\*170#**, **\*110#**, a bank app, or Ewale **\*714\*22#** / [ewalepay.com](https://ewalepay.com), the discipline is the same. Confirm name, one debit unless reversed, then wait on that debit’s token. Preferred everyday checkout here is **\*714\*22#** because you are already watching one SMS thread for a name-checked purchase — not because it is magically faster than ECG’s own USSD in all cells.

## A realistic clock, not a SLA

Many purchases text within a minute or two. Some take longer when vending or SMS gateways are busy — evenings, heatwave nights, after a widespread outage when everyone vends at once. “Five minutes feels like failure” is a feeling. “Thirty minutes with a confirmed debit and empty inboxes on both SIMs” is a support case.

There is no honest public promise that every rail is identical. PowerApp may show the token on-screen before SMS. USSD may only SMS. Web receipts may display digits you should still save as SMS or screenshot. If the interface shows the token, you do not have to wait on SMS to start typing. If it does not, you wait.

## What to do while you wait

Check wallet: pending versus completed. Check notifications. Check the SIM you used for USSD and the SIM you use for MoMo. Toggle airplane mode once if you like; then stop looping it. Do not open a second checkout.

Call waiting and a full inbox delay SMS more than people admit. Delete old bank OTPs if the SIM is choking, after you are sure you will not delete the token that just arrived.

If PowerApp login is what you are waiting on, that is OTP, not a vend. **\*713\*414#** may show login OTP quickly. It will not hurry a prepaid token.

## When to call 0302 611 611

When there is a completed debit, a reference, no token on any interface or SMS after a patient wait, and you have not already issued a second payment. Give them the meter number, time, amount, and reference from MoMo or the checkout. Care is for missing vends, not for “I have not looked at SIM 2.”

If you paid via Ewale or a bank, their support plus ECG may both be needed. Bring both references. Do not rant that “226 is faster” as a substitute for the ID.

## Second purchases destroy the timeline

A late first token plus a second token is two valid-looking strings and a house still dark if nobody typed. Enter what you have. Some meters treat an unused older token as Old after a newer one is accepted — do not shuffle randomly. Overpaying is painful; it is not proof the first wait “did not count.”

PIN timeout without debit: wait was irrelevant, you may buy once. Timeout with debit: you are already on the clock of the first token.

## Channel folklore

People swear **\*226#** is instant and web is slow, or the reverse. Cells differ. Your story from last month is not a benchmark. The cheap experiment is a small vend on a lit afternoon on your primary code so you know *your* typical delay.

Vendor tills can print immediately because the printer is in the room. That speed is not extra units. It is a different display. Online is SMS-shaped.

## Pending, reversed, and “it came after I bought again”

Pending is not permission to open **\*170#**. If pending becomes success, the token is on the way. If pending becomes fail or reverse, then you may buy once. Until the wallet speaks, you wait.

The cruel case is: you wait twenty minutes, buy again on **\*714\*22#**, then both SMSes arrive. Enter both on the meter if they are unused. You overpaid. You did not prove the first channel was broken. Next time, call **0302 611 611** with the first reference instead of a second PIN.

PowerApp can show a token in Transactions while SMS is still walking. Look there before you declare delay. USSD users look at SMS and wallet only — that is fine; it is not slower vending, it is fewer screens.

## After the SMS, the clock is yours

Typing slowly at the meter is allowed. The token does not expire in thirty seconds like a PIN prompt. Used/invalid/old errors are about the meter state and the string, not about you waiting four minutes to walk down the corridor.

If neighbours are also dark, adding speed will not lift a feeder. You can still vend; lights wait on the network.

A rehearsal vend on a calm afternoon tells you whether your SIM usually sees SMS in one minute or in ten. That personal baseline is more useful than a stranger’s “it is always instant on \*226#.” If your baseline is ten minutes, do not panic at minute two.

Token delay is an SMS-and-queue story. History tells you whether to wait or to recover. Save **\*714\*22#** so the wait happens on a channel you can explain. Keep **\*226#** as official USSD. Keep one debit. The minutes you wait are cheaper than the minutes you spend buying twice.
