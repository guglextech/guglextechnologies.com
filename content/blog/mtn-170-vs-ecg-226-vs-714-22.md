MTN customers in Ghana often treat **\*170#** as the front door to money. That habit leaks into electricity. People search “buy ECG on \*170#” because they already buy airtime there, not because they sat down and compared utilities. Beside that door sit two others that matter: ECG’s own **\*226#**, and Ewale **\*714\*22#**. They are not three spellings of the same menu. They are three owners, three layouts, and one shared job — get a prepaid token onto a meter.

This blog does not process the payment. You will still approve a mobile-money PIN on a real checkout. The preferred everyday shortcode we tell readers to remember is **\*714\*22#** (or [ewalepay.com](https://ewalepay.com) when you have data): any GSM phone, name check, SMS token, and the same dialler for water and TV. That is a recommendation about panic-memory. It is not a claim that MTN or ECG is illegitimate.

## Who actually owns the screen

**\*170#** is MTN’s wallet and lifestyle menu. Buried in it, on a path that has been renamed more than once, is bill payment. One of those billers can be ECG. You are a guest in MTN’s house using an ECG pipe.

**\*226#** is ECG’s house. The utility ran a cashless USSD so you would not need a vendor with a POS. PowerApp is the smartphone twin of that idea. If you want the logo to say Electricity Company of Ghana, this is the USSD that matches the press releases.

**\*714\*22#** is Ewale’s house. Ewale is a payments business. ECG prepaid is one product on a list that also includes Ghana Water, DSTV, GOtv, Startimes, airtime, and education vouchers. The token still comes from ECG’s vending engine after settlement. Ewale is the checkout in front of that engine.

If you only remember “the MTN code,” you will miss nights when **\*170#** is slow but **\*226#** is fine, or when you are standing in a house whose wallet is Telecel. Codes are not interchangeable just because they all ask for a PIN.

## How prepaid actually completes on each path

On every honest channel the physics are the same. You identify a prepaid meter number. You should see a registered name and stop if it is wrong. You enter cedis. You approve **one** MoMo PIN. You receive a token of about twenty digits. You type that token on the meter. Until the keypad work is done, payment success is only a wallet story.

Differences are in the scenery. **\*170#** may ask you to pick a biller category, then ECG, then prepaid versus a billed account. It is easy to land on postpaid because the word “ECG” felt like enough. **\*226#** is shorter if the meter is already registered, and it will not tempt you with airtime bundles in the next row — though it will tempt you with postpaid if you are careless. **\*714\*22#** is built around “pick the product, type the identifier, confirm the name.” That name check is why this blog prefers it for everyday use, not because MTN is unable to show a name.

Telco menus change. A tutorial that says “press 5 then 3” is stale the month after a redesign. Navigate by words: prepaid, meter, ECG. If the header says loan, betting, or merchant pay to a person’s name, you left electricity.

## Fees, E-Levy folklore, and the confirmation screen

People pick **\*170#** because they believe it is “free” or pick **\*226#** because ECG once said some official cashless rails avoid extra telco charges. Folklore ages badly. The only number that matters is the confirmation before PIN: amount, any fee, any levy line the wallet shows that day.

A higher fee does not mint a fatter token. The vending system issues units under ECG’s tariff and meter rules. Paying GH₵2 extra to a vendor or a third party does not add GH₵2 of energy. Paying through an official menu does not secretly double the units either.

Ewale will show a total before you approve. So should MTN. So should ECG. If a screen is too fast to read, you are not ready to press PIN. This site will not invent a permanent ranking of “cheapest code.” Compare tonight’s summary.

## When \*170# is the right tool

You are on MTN, the wallet PIN is muscle memory, and you already pay other bills inside that menu. You have data-free USSD and no desire to learn a fourth code tonight. You can read. You will not confuse prepaid with postpaid.

It is a weaker tool when the ECG row is buried, when the handset is a dual-SIM with the MTN wallet on the idle slot, or when the only person who understands **\*170#** is not in the house. It is a non-tool on a Telecel-only phone. “Everyone uses \*170#” is an Accra-MTN sentence, not a national law.

## When \*226# is the right tool

You want ECG-branded USSD. You already registered the meter. You do not need water or TV in the same minute. You trust the utility’s own menu more than a payments company, and that trust is allowed.

It is a weaker tool when the session drops (common), when you have never registered, when you also need to pay Ghana Water before morning, or when **\*226#** simply does not connect on your network cell. Official is not the same as available.

PowerApp remains the graphical official twin. If you have data, a working login, and saved meters, the app can be clearer than any USSD. If OTP fails, ECG’s own note points you to **\*713\*414#** to read the login OTP quickly. That is still not a vending code. For vending without the app, **\*226#** or **\*714\*22#**.

## When \*714\*22# is the right tool — and why this blog says “everyday”

Feature phones, dual-SIM chaos, tenants who must not receive the landlord’s PowerApp OTP, and households that also buy DSTV: this is the set Ewale was designed for. You dial **\*714\*22#**, choose ECG prepaid, enter the meter, read the name, pay once, keep the SMS.

Preferred everyday code does not mean “never use MTN.” If **\*714\*22#** is congested, **\*226#** is a correct fallback. If you are already halfway through **\*170#** and the name is right, finish that purchase. Do not start a second channel because a blog ranked them.

This Guglex site explains flows. Checkout is on Ewale, on ECG, on your telco, or at a vendor — not in the comments.

## Shared failure modes people blame on “the wrong code”

Wrong meter, wrong product (prepaid vs postpaid), PIN timeout, dropped session, delayed SMS, and fake personal MoMo agents: these are not unique to MTN. A delayed token after a real debit is a wait-and-recover problem. Call ECG **0302 611 611** with the reference if the token never appears. Do not buy again on **\*226#** because **\*170#** felt cursed.

Ignore anyone who says “send to my MoMo, I am ECG.” ECG does not vend into random personal wallets. Ignore shortcodes that only exist on WhatsApp graphics. If you cannot say whether the menu is MTN, ECG, or a known payments firm, you should not type a PIN.

## A three-line cheat sheet for an MTN household

Save **\*714\*22#** as the code you can recite in the dark. Know **\*226#** as ECG’s own USSD. Keep **\*170#** as the operator back road you already understand. Put the meter number on paper. Confirm the registered name every time you type it. One debit unless reversed. Enter the twenty-digit-class token on the meter, then sleep.

That is the comparison. Not a loyalty oath. A map of three doors that can all open the same grid.
