Search logs and compound WhatsApp groups still produce the query “ECG 718” or “\*718\* for light.” Sometimes the person once used a telco path that lived near 718. Sometimes a vendor’s sticker from another year survived a move. Sometimes a graphic that said “new ECG code” used a number that looked official. The result is the same: a human in the dark trying a string that opens the wrong menu, or no menu, then accepting a personal MoMo agent as the grown-up option.

Electricity Company of Ghana’s official USSD is **\*226#**. The official smartphone app is PowerApp. MTN customers often reach bill-pay through **\*170#**. Telecel and AT customers often use **\*110#**. This blog’s preferred everyday recitable code is Ewale **\*714\*22#** (and [ewalepay.com](https://ewalepay.com) with data): name check, SMS token of about twenty digits, keypad phone friendly, also water and TV. This site does not debit you. A 718 rumour does not vend you.

## Shortcodes are not permanent street names

Telcos reuse and retire USSD. Banks do too. Payments firms do too. A number that bought a utility in 2018 may now be a loan, a promo, or nothing. Teaching “718 is ECG” as folklore is how people donate PINs to whatever currently sits on that code.

Even **\*170#** and **\*110#** keep the parent number while moving the ECG row around inside. The parent is a mall. The shop moves. If your memory is only three digits, you will buy airtime.

**\*226#** has been ECG’s public cashless USSD identity in consumer education. If you need one official utility number to store besides your everyday payments code, store that. Not a cousin you half-remember.

## The 713 confusion sitting beside 718

People mix digits. **\*713\*414#** is documented in ECG PowerApp help as an OTP viewer if the login SMS is late and you are still inside a short window. It is a real code with a narrow job. It is not 718, and it is not a place to buy prepaid. Fridge labels that say “ECG: 713 or 718 or 226” are how tenants try three wrong shops and one right one, then double-pay the right one out of shame.

If you are not in the middle of a PowerApp login, you have no business dialling the OTP viewer.

## How fake “updated 2026 codes” use your nostalgia

Scam flyers need a hook that feels like insider knowledge. “ECG has changed the code from 226 to 718” is a perfect sentence: it explains why your honest attempt failed (maybe the session just dropped) and it pushes you onto their USSD or their wallet. ECG does not announce a replacement official USSD via a stranger’s status.

If **\*226#** does not connect, that is often radio or congestion. Next honest doors: PowerApp if login works, **\*170#** / **\*110#** bill-pay, **\*714\*22#**, a bank’s ECG biller, a licensed till. Not a personal number named “ECG 718 vendor.”

ECG customer care **0302 611 611** can confirm process after a real debit. It will not bless a WhatsApp shortcode.

## What to type instead of hunting 718

- Want ECG’s own USSD: **\*226#**.
- Want a smartphone history of meters: PowerApp.
- Want MTN’s familiar menu: **\*170#**, then words that say ECG prepaid, then name check.
- Want Telecel/AT native: **\*110#**, same discipline.
- Want one code a feature phone can do for light, water, and DSTV: **\*714\*22#**.

Always confirm the registered name. Always one PIN unless the debit is reversed. Always enter the token on the meter. Always ignore P2P “agents.”

## If 718 still opens something on your phone

Read the header like a sceptic. If it is a loan, a game, a merchant pay to a person, or a token generator asking for your PIN in-chat, hang up. If it is a telco menu that can still reach an ECG biller, you got lucky on an old habit — but save the real biller path, not the folklore number.

Do not teach 718 to the next tenant. Teach the meter number, the expected name, **\*714\*22#**, and **\*226#**.

## 170, 110, 226, 714 — why three digits lie

People remember three digits because USSD folklore is oral. **\*170#** is three digits and is not ECG. **\*110#** is three digits and is not ECG. **\*226#** is three digits and is ECG. **\*714\*22#** is longer on purpose — it is a payments shortcode, not a telco home. Searching 718 is the brain trying to complete a three-digit pattern that never mapped cleanly onto electricity.

If you must tattoo three digits, tattoo 226 as the utility USSD, and still write 714\*22 for everyday mixed bills. Do not tattoo 718.

Voice notes from uncles that begin “try 718, if not 713…” are how OTP viewers get sold as shops. Interrupt the voice note with a name check on a real channel.

## Why search exists even when the answer is stable

People search when the lights are already low. They type whatever fragment they remember. Journals and blogs that chase “718” as a keyword without saying it is a dead end keep the loop alive. This article exists to break the loop: 718 is a memory, not a method.

The method is a named owner, a meter lookup, a name, one debit, a ~20-digit token, keypad, units. **\*226#** and **\*714\*22#** both know that method. Mystery strings do not.

Stop searching for the ghost code. Save the living ones. If a cousin swears 718 still works, ask them to show the header and the name check on a meter they own — on a lit afternoon, not as a sermon in your blackout.
